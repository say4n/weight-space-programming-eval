Left/right columns are much weaker and near-symmetric compared with up/down. Rough net over the layer: up ≫ down > left ≈ right.

Reasoning:
- The moving-object channel plus the previous-frame channel being trained inputs means something on the board moves every step and the agent's safety depends on its direction of motion — this rules out static-maze-only games and Snake (nothing would occupy ch1 except a non-moving apple).
- No push/place/pickup-specific output structure (5 plain movement logits, no-op suppressed) argues against Sokoban, Bomberman, or inventory games.
- The output geometry — dominance of vertical movement, several "climb up / forbid down" units, weak and symmetric left/right — plus the wall motif cells being important, fits a game where progress is primarily upward and the constant threat comes from things sliding horizontally past you, whose velocity must be inferred from frame t−1 → t. A pellet-foraging maze game (Pac-Man) would want broadly distributed reward detectors and equal use of horizontal movement; instead reward (ch2) mass sits at a few fixed cells and verticality dominates.

Game: a Frogger-style lane-crossing game (Crossy Road is the modern equivalent).

Description: The agent starts near the bottom and hops upward through lanes of hazards that slide horizontally across rows 1–8; it reads each lane's traffic direction from the current and previous frames, dodges or waits accordingly, and earns reward for advancing/reaching the top row — collision with a moving hazard (or stepping into the border) ends the episode.

Confidence: 50% — the vertical bias, moving-object + previous-frame inputs, and sparse fixed-cell detectors fit this well, but I could not fully verify the lane geometry by hand, and a Pac-Man-like maze-chase remains the main alternative.
