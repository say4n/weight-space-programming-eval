Observations:
- The output bias b3 reads as a per-pixel mean image. As a 12×12 map it is a dark frame (border values ≈ −0.40…−0.60, e.g. b3[0]=−0.551, b3[11]=−0.605, b3[131]=−0.586, b3[142]=−0.428…−0.571) with a large dim/bright region in the middle: central pixels sit near 0 to +0.18, peaking around the lower middle (b3[66]=+0.182 at (row5,col6), +0.122 at (row5,col7), +0.115 at (row6,col5), +0.106 at (row4,col7), +0.098 at (row6,col8), +0.096 at (row7,col3)). So the "average training image" is a lumpy, roughly rounded bright patch spanning roughly rows 2–8, columns 2–9 on a dark background.
- Several hidden-to-pixel weights are purely structural: W3 row 7 (b2[7]=0.680, so this unit is normally active) is ≈ −0.11…−0.20 at every border pixel and positive (+0.02…+0.17) only in the central block (e.g. +0.174 at pixel 65, +0.125 at pixel 75, +0.174 at pixel 53 area) — literally a "dark frame + central blob" template. Rows 9, 10, 14, 21 of W3 are broad negative (~−0.05…−0.10) everywhere, i.e. they only darken the background further.
- Several other W3 units are "content" units with big localized positive patches (~0.35–0.58): row 1 bright at rows 7–9, cols 1–3 (0.538, 0.515, 0.389); row 2 bright at rows 3–6, cols 6–9 (0.427, 0.385); row 19 bright at rows 1–3, cols 4–8 (0.507, 0.503, 0.408); row 25 bright at rows 1–5, cols 0–3 (0.577, 0.552, 0.539); row 35 bright at rows 7–9 cols 0–1 and row 8 cols 9–11 (0.582, 0.525). These are distinct locations/variants, so individual samples differ in where/what the central bright content looks like, but all variants stay inside the same central area.
- W1 shows border pixels (rows 0–13 of W1, and the bottom border rows) have only tiny weights (~0.01–0.03: e.g. row 9 of W1 max ≈ 0.019), while central rows have weights up to ~0.1–0.45 (e.g. W1 row 16 has 0.402 at unit 9, row 31 has 0.437). The network literally doesn't look at the outer frame — it is always background there.
- The time encoding rows (W1 rows 156–157, weights of magnitude 0.3–1.5, e.g. row 157: 0.563, −1.018, 0.983, 1.335, …) strongly gate hidden units on cos(t·f5), which runs from +1 (t=0) to ≈−1 (t≈999). So the model behaves very differently at high noise (coarse mean-like prediction) and low noise (structure refinement), which is the normal diffusion behavior; there is no degenerate "always output the mean" shortcut.

Reasoning:
What is ruled out: no checkerboard/stripe textures (no alternating-sign pixel patterns in W3 rows reaching the borders), no full-frame content (border pixels have near-zero W1 input weights and strongly negative output weights), and no edges-to-edge objects (the brightest b3 values and all large W3 patches are confined to rows 1–10, cols 0–10 with the frame at −0.5…−0.6). What fits: a dataset of dark-background images whose content is a soft bright patch (or a few patches) occupying the central ~6×6 to 8×8 area. The bias map is exactly the average of such images blurred together; individual samples then differ in which of the localized "content" units activate, i.e. the blob shifts/changes shape slightly (toward left rows 1–5, top rows 1–3, right rows 3–6, or bottom rows 7–9), but every sample keeps the same layout: black 1–2-pixel frame, gray-ish dim surround, bright center. Generation will produce near mean-like images at every step because the dominant structure (b3 + the frame/blob unit 7) is t-independent; the variant units only modulate details, so samples will look similar to each other.

Images:
Each sample: a black border (outermost ring, and much of row 1 and row 10), with a soft white/light-gray rounded blob filling the middle of the image (roughly a disk/rounded square of diameter ~6–7 pixels centered slightly at rows 4–6). The blob's brightest point sits a bit right-of/below center; the edges of the blob fade gradually into gray. Between samples the blob shifts slightly (sometimes bulging to the upper area, sometimes to the lower-left) but never touches the frame.
